# Track A (Healthy Nation: Caring for an Ageing Singapore) — dataset availability and feasibility for A1 SilverWatch, A2 DengueRadar, A3 HealthPulse

Method note: On 2026-09-23 I pulled the full data.gov.sg catalogue through the public v2 API (`https://api-production.data.gov.sg/v2/public/api/datasets?page=N`). That gave 4,629 datasets over 463 pages. I then sampled every relevant dataset with `https://data.gov.sg/api/action/datastore_search?resource_id=<id>` for row totals and column names. GeoJSON files came from `https://api-open.data.gov.sg/v1/public/api/datasets/<id>/poll-download`. I also made live calls to the v2 real-time weather API. Dataset IDs, row totals, columns and coverage below come from those live calls unless marked otherwise. Dataset page URLs follow the pattern `https://data.gov.sg/datasets/<datasetId>/view`.

Problem text: `docs/problem-statements/track-A_healthy-nation_ageing.txt`. Constraints: Databricks Free Edition is serverless only, with one 2X-Small SQL warehouse, 5 concurrent job tasks, 1 pipeline per type, 3 apps and daily quotas. Judges score "Data rigour 10%" as "honest limitations" and "Technical feasibility 25%" as "buildable in two weeks" (`docs/guidelines/judging-criteria.txt`, `databricks-free-edition_setup_and_limits.txt`).

## Cross-cutting: data.gov.sg API access, rate limits and planning-area geometry

### Takeaway
The data.gov.sg APIs work without a login, but rate limits are tight without an API key. Get a free key. Planning-area and subzone boundaries (MP2019 and MP2025) are available as GeoJSON with PA names that join to Census 2020 tables.

### Cited Findings

Enforcement started 31 Dec 2025 — [data.gov.sg: Upgrade to API Keys](https://guide.data.gov.sg/whats-new/extended-deadline-upgrade-to-api-keys-by-dec-31-2025).

### Inferences
- Joining on uppercase PA name (Census "Ang Mo Kio - Total" to GeoJSON "ANG MO KIO") needs some cleaning, but it's routine work that fits the "handling messy data" criterion.
- If the SingStat June-2011..2025 planning-area file can be downloaded, it is the most valuable extra dataset for both A1 and A3. It would give roughly 15 annual points of PA×age×sex population to trend-extrapolate for 2030 projections. Without it, teams only have 2010, 2015 (GHS) and 2020 snapshots.

### Gaps
- I could not open the SingStat annual PA/subzone file directly (404 on fetch). Confirm the download link manually before relying on it.
- I did not find official population *projections* at planning-area level. They look unpublished; only national projections exist. Any 2030 PA projection will have to be team-built, for example by cohort-ageing Census 2020 5-year bands or trend-extrapolating the annual June files.

## A1 SilverWatch — elderly social isolation risk by planning area

### Takeaway
A1 is buildable, but the problem statement oversells its data. "HDB Elderly & Future-Elderly Population" is **town-level survey data (latest 2018), not block-level**. The living-alone data for people aged 65+ is **national only**. The AAC/SAC data is a **national count** plus a **2016 point layer with no capacity field**. ComCare is **national only**. There is no ground truth for isolation. A composite index at PA level is still achievable with Census 2020 proxies, such as one-person households, households with no family nucleus, 65+ population and households with ADL difficulty. It has to be presented as an unvalidated heuristic index, or weights need to be set by expert/AHP judgement.

### Inferences
- **Winning-demo items, one by one:**
  1. *Heatmap of PAs by composite score*: feasible, using Census 2020 PA tables and MP2019 boundaries.
  2. *"ML model combining elderly density, living-alone rates and AAC coverage gaps"*: PA-level living-alone for 65+ does not exist, so the team has to use a proxy. One option is PA one-person-household share × PA 65+ share. Another is to calibrate with national 65+ living-alone ratios by age and sex applied to PA age/sex structure (indirect standardisation / small-area estimation). The honest framing is an unsupervised composite index (z-score/PCA/AHP), or a small-area estimate. A supervised ML model has no label to learn from.
  3. *AAC coverage gap*: this needs current AAC points, and the open layer is from 2016 with no capacity. The team would either geocode AAC addresses from AIC/SupportGoWhere (scrape or manual list, around 250 rows) or use the 2016 layer and say so. Capacity isn't published, so coverage = 65+ residents within 400 m/800 m walking distance per centre. Terms of use for scraping AIC were not checked.
  4. *Top-10 list and dashboard*: easy on Databricks AI/BI.
- **Validation:** there is no PA-level isolation ground truth. Teams could cross-check against PA rates of "households with ADL difficulty" or 1–2-room rental density, which are correlated but not true labels. Sensitivity analysis of the weights also counts as rigour.
- **2030 stretch:** feasible by cohort-ageing Census 2020 PA 5-year bands (the 60–64 band becomes 65–69 and so on, with mortality ignored or applied nationally). A cleaner route is trend-extrapolating the SingStat annual June PA files, if they can be obtained.
- **Feasibility in 2 weeks on Free Edition:** HIGH for the data engineering and dashboard. The data is small (hundreds of rows) and fits comfortably in quota. Scientific credibility is MEDIUM because the core signals are proxies. The data is static, so no live streaming "wow" beyond batch ingestion.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether SingStat publishes any table of 65+ by living arrangement **by planning area**. I searched data.gov.sg exhaustively and found none. The SingStat Census 2020 Statistical Release PDF was not reviewed.
- I did not verify whether AIC's E-care Locator has a machine-readable endpoint or permits scraping.
- There is no open ComCare or Silver Support data by area on data.gov.sg. MSF may publish area-level figures in reports, but I did not verify this.

## A2 DengueRadar — dengue outbreak forecasting by planning area

### Takeaway
Weather data is excellent. There are historical CSVs for 2016–2024, and the v2 real-time API accepts a `date=` parameter that returned past days back to at least 2019 in my tests. **Dengue history is the blocker.** "Dengue Cases by Region" is really five 2023 snapshot GeoJSONs, not a time series. The Dengue Clusters GeoJSON is **current-only**. Official weekly counts on data.gov.sg are **national and end at 2022-W52**. A credible 2-week forecast is only achievable at **national weekly level** with open data. A planning-area Low/Medium/High map has to come from a national forecast downscaled with current clusters and Aedes layers, or the team builds on the SGCharts 2013–2020 cluster archive.

### Cited Findings
**Dengue:**
- **Dengue Clusters (GEOJSON)**, NEA, `d_dbfabf16158d1b0e1c420627c0819168`. It is **current snapshot only**, with catalogue coverage starting 2025-11 and daily updates (last updated 2026-09-23). The download held 13 polygons with fields `LOCALITY`, `CASE_SIZE`, `HOMES`, `PUBLIC_PLACES`, `CONSTRUCTION_SITES` and `FMEL_UPD_D` (e.g. "Canberra Rd (Blk 308) / Sembawang Vista (Blk 318)", CASE_SIZE 2). I found no historical versions through the API — [data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_dbfabf16158d1b0e1c420627c0819168/view)
- **"Dengue Cases by Region"** is actually five GeoJSONs, **Dengue (Cases) – Central / North East / North West / South East / South West**, e.g. Central `d_5f90123ce50e3d323bfd0ff3c9a84601`. Central held 44 small polygons each labelled "No. of Dengue Cases : N", with timestamps from **Feb–Mar 2023**. The catalogue shows no updates since 2024-06-06. It is a **stale point-in-time snapshot, not historical weekly counts** — [data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_5f90123ce50e3d323bfd0ff3c9a84601/view)
- **Weekly Infectious Disease Bulletin Cases**, MOH, `d_ca168b2cb763640d72c4600a68f9909e`: 20,070 rows with columns `epi_week, disease, no._of_cases`, covering 2012-W01 to **2022-W52**. Dengue Fever and DHF make up 1,148 rows, e.g. 2022-W52 Dengue Fever = 285. **National only** — [data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_ca168b2cb763640d72c4600a68f9909e/view)
- **Weekly Number of Dengue and Dengue Haemorrhagic Fever Cases**, `d_ac1eecf0886ff0bceefbc51556247015`: 530 rows, 2014–2018, national — [data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_ac1eecf0886ff0bceefbc51556247015/view)
- **Dengue Outbreak Statistics**, `d_8763ae810003718ad638e719ec9118df`: 9 rows of annual incidence rate, 2007–2015 — [data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_8763ae810003718ad638e719ec9118df/view)
- Weekly bulletins for 2023 onward now come from the Communicable Diseases Agency (CDA). The 2026 page offers PDFs plus a downloadable Excel file, with EW36 published 17 Sep 2026. Only 2026 is listed on that page — [CDA Weekly Infectious Diseases Bulletin 2026](https://www.cda.gov.sg/resources/weekly-infectious-diseases-bulletin-2026/)
- NEA's Quarterly Dengue Surveillance Data is **PDF-only** and runs from Q1 2018 to Q2 2026 — [NEA](https://www.nea.gov.sg/dengue-zika/dengue/quarterly-dengue-surveillance-data)
- Sub-national history exists only in a third-party archive. SGCharts Outbreak holds NEA cluster snapshots taken twice weekly from **May 2013 to Nov 2020**, then stopped. That's 250+ snapshots with address, latitude/longitude, cluster number, recent cases and total cases, in PDF and CSV — [SGCharts Outbreak data](https://outbreak.sgcharts.com/data)
- An academic study (arXiv 2601.12856) used the SGCharts archive for 2013–2018 and 2020 at **subzone** level, with 323 subzones and one-week-ahead hotspot forecasting. It reported mean F-scores of 0.71–0.90 but **no baseline comparison**. Code: github.com/lphuang2022/dengue-hotspot-forecasting-and-spreading-network-recognition — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12856)
- **Areas with High Aedes Population (GEOJSON)**, `d_5d060d8b7838a15e8906fb22c50dbf51`: coverage from 2025-10, updated 2026-08-29, current snapshot. **Aedes Mosquito Breeding Habitats** (5 regions) are stale 2024 snapshots — [data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_5d060d8b7838a15e8906fb22c50dbf51/view)

**Weather:**
- **Rainfall across Singapore**, real-time API, `d_...` family; endpoint `https://api-open.data.gov.sg/v2/real-time/api/rainfall`. A live call returned 89 stations at 5-minute resolution. `?date=2021-06-15` returned **historical** readings from 69 stations, paginated at 25 readings per page with a `paginationToken`. `?date=2026-09-01T10:00:00` returned a single timestamp. - **Air Temperature across Singapore**, real-time API, `d_66b77726bbae1b33f218db60ff5861f0`, coverage from 2016-05. `?date=2019-03-01` returned 17 stations with historical readings — [data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_66b77726bbae1b33f218db60ff5861f0/view)
- **Historical Rainfall across Singapore (2016–2024)** consists of one CSV per year: 2017 `d_1990a5a1aeaf3dd243cf4dae294a61c4`, 2018 `d_024fb501ce7092b71bb713eaf54fa7eb`, 2019 `d_61995f092320e7155b7528050880b502`, 2020 `d_9e7de44094f876f6804b8b5bcee45c81`, 2021 `d_3b41598f74f1f11fc3430348fea51af5`, 2022 `d_42d64cc6c176ace1c52fbb40b9ede302`, 2023 `d_f864cc30d58b467db83659ad17c737bf`, 2024 `d_a0b69d3e02576a1fd0ab673e71f83507`. **The "2016" file covers only 2016-12-02 to 12-03.** The collection carries a disclaimer that it "may contain missing records and has not undergone quality control" — [data.gov.sg Historical Rainfall 2020](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_9e7de44094f876f6804b8b5bcee45c81/view)
- **Historical Air Temperature across Singapore** exists per year for 2016–2024 (e.g. 2024 `d_910c81fce47f38574015b3882ac59254`), and Historical Relative Humidity for 2016 onward — [data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_910c81fce47f38574015b3882ac59254/view)
- **Air Temperature And Sunshine, Relative Humidity And Rainfall, Monthly**, SingStat `d_1744ca68dc…`: 1975-01 to **2026-07**, a national/Changi series — [data.gov.sg catalogue](https://data.gov.sg/datasets?query=air%20temperature%20and%20sunshine)
- Environmental proxies: **Hawker Centres (GEOJSON)** `d_4a086da0a5553be1d89383cd90d07ecd` (current), **NParks Parks and Nature Reserves** `d_77d7ec97be83d44f61b85454f844382f`, **Parks** `d_0542d48f0991541706b58059381a6eca` — [data.gov.sg catalogue](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_4a086da0a5553be1d89383cd90d07ecd/view)

### Inferences
- **Historical weather via API:** yes, it works. Backfilling 2025–2026, which has no yearly CSV yet, costs about 12 pages per day per variable. For roughly 630 days that's about 7,500 calls. At 6 calls per 10 s without a key, that's around 3.5 hours; with a Dev key, around 1.7 hours. Use the CSVs for 2017–2024 and the API only for 2025 onward, aggregated to daily or weekly.
- **Dengue target series:** national weekly counts stitched together from data.gov.sg (2012–2022) plus CDA bulletins (2023–2026, Excel/PDF). This is enough for a national 2-week-ahead model with weather lags of 2–12 weeks, benchmarked against a naive or seasonal-naive / 4-week moving-average baseline. **This is credible and doable in 2 weeks.**
- **Planning-area forecast:** there is no open PA-level case time series for 2021–2026. Options:
  - (a) Train a PA/subzone hotspot model on SGCharts 2013–2020 cluster points. This is a real sub-national history, but it's third-party, ends in 2020, and doesn't connect to the live feed.
  - (b) Allocate the national forecast to PAs using current-cluster intensity, Aedes-high areas and static risk features, then classify Low/Medium/High. This is explainable, but the "beats baseline" claim can only be made at national level.
  - (c) Archive the live cluster GeoJSON daily during the hackathon. That gives about 14 snapshots, which is fine for the real-time map but useless for training.
  Recommend (b) plus an optional (a), with limitations stated clearly.
- The real-time cluster map and incremental ingestion (a scheduled job polling cluster GeoJSON and 5-minute weather) are easy and show off Lakeflow / Auto Loader / Jobs well. With 5 concurrent job tasks there is enough headroom.
- **Feasibility:** HIGH for the pipeline and national model. MEDIUM-LOW for a *validated* planning-area forecast. It is the most "technical-execution" friendly of the three problems and has the clearest baseline story.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether the CDA site hosts downloadable Excel bulletins for 2023–2025, only 2026. If not, those weeks would have to be pulled from PDFs.
- I could not confirm whether NEA publishes weekly cases by the five regions anywhere as a time series. The NEA dengue-clusters web page may show regional counts for the current week only.
- I didn't find the exact dataset ID for the Rainfall real-time API. The endpoint works regardless.
- I didn't check SGCharts licensing or CSV completeness in detail. It says it is "preserved for research purposes" and asks for attribution to NEA.

## A3 HealthPulse — 5-year community healthcare demand forecasting by planning area

### Takeaway
There is no open sub-national health **demand** data by planning area. Admissions are national, though the monthly series is broken down **by public hospital**. Beds are available by **planning region**, 5 regions, for 2020–2025. Facility counts are national, except for **CHAS clinic points (2021)** and **polyclinic points**. There are **no nursing-home point locations** on data.gov.sg. Planning-area "demand" therefore has to be modelled: PA population by age (Census 2020, plus annual SingStat files if available) × national age-specific utilisation rates (hospital admission rate by age and sex). That approach is standard and defensible, but it is population-projection-driven rather than driven by granular demand signals.

### Cited Findings
- **Admissions To Public Sector Hospitals, Monthly**, `d_1338b55f6d4ea6b2df9884ec4bce4464`, SingStat: 15 rows, **1987-01 to 2026-07**, with one row per **public hospital**. ### Inferences
- **PA-level 5-year forecast:** do this as small-area demand estimation. First, project PA population by 5-year age and sex to 2031 by cohort-ageing Census 2020, calibrated to regional End-June 2019–2025 totals, or use the annual SingStat PA files if obtained. Second, multiply by national age-specific admission rates, trended with a time-series model on 2006–2024. Third, reconcile against national monthly admissions (1987–2026). Hospital-level monthly admissions allow a sanity check of *catchment* demand, e.g. Changi General ≈ East. This is a defensible "top-down plus bottom-up" methodology, but it is not a granular demand signal. Be honest that PA variation comes from demographics only.
- **Supply side:** CHAS GP points (2021) and polyclinic points can be counted per PA with a spatial join. Nursing homes, community hospitals and AAC/day care have **no open point layer on data.gov.sg**, only national or regional counts. Teams would need to geocode ~80 nursing homes by hand from AIC/MOH lists, or use regional beds only.
- **Top-5 underserved map:** feasible for **primary care** (GP and polyclinic per 1,000 elderly). For nursing homes it is only possible at regional level unless locations are hand-geocoded.
- **Healthier SG scenario toggles:** this can only be done as assumed % reductions in age-specific rates, since there is no open programme outcome data.
- **Feasibility:** HIGH for the pipeline and dashboard, and the data is small. MEDIUM for credibility, because the "forecast" is mostly demographic projection and the target (PA demand) can't be validated. It's the most "policy-planning" friendly story, but its ML component is the weakest of the three.

### Gaps
- I did not find official PA-level population projections, polyclinic catchment utilisation, or a nursing-home point layer on data.gov.sg. I didn't check whether OneMap themes ("eldercare", "nursing homes") expose them, because a search turned up no documentation.
- The CHAS Clinics layer is a 2021 snapshot. I didn't check whether a fresher CHAS/HAS clinic list exists in the "Health Appointment System – mapping from clinics to offerings" dataset (`d_d3fc278691129cdc73a191cb2575a495`, 2024).

## Comparative buildability judgement (2 weeks, student team, Databricks Free Edition)

### Takeaway
All three are buildable as pipelines and dashboards because the data is small. What separates them is how honestly each can deliver its "winning demo". **A2** has the strongest technical and ML story: live APIs, a real time series and a real baseline test. But its planning-area granularity has to be modelled or downscaled. **A1** has the strongest social-impact narrative, with easy PA-level Census proxies, but rests on proxies with no ground truth and stale AAC locations. **A3** has the weakest data fit: demand is national, supply is regional or national, and its "forecast" is mainly demographic projection.
